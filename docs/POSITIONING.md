# Positioning and Messaging

The high-level messaging spine for Digital Fusion, and the frame every feature
has to fit into. Nothing on the site should say anything that does not ladder
back to one of the four answers below.

**Status:** first pass, 09 September 2026. Recommendations, not sign-off.
Sources are the Brand Book v1.1 and seven app screenshots dated 09 September
2026. Open decisions are collected at the end, and the changelog is at the very
bottom. Update this document rather than starting a new one.

---

## Q1. The one thing we can do that no one else can

> **You buy frontier AI the way you buy airtime, and every answer tells you
> what it cost.**

Two halves, and neither works alone. Prepaid on its own is just a payment
method. Cost transparency on its own is a settings screen. Together they are a
different product category, and the reason no competitor can follow is
structural rather than technical.

A subscription business cannot show you what each answer cost. The entire
proposition of a monthly fee is that the unit cost stays invisible, because a
heavy user is subsidised by a light one and revealing the per-answer figure
breaks the arrangement in both directions. So the competition is not choosing
to hide it. They cannot show it and stay a subscription.

That is the moat. Not the technology, not the model access, not the price. The
business model.

### What holds this up

Prepaid, in rand, no card. A R20 bundle bought like airtime, with no
subscription and no monthly debit order. The alternatives sell dollar
subscriptions from about R149 to R324 a month, on cards that take
international recurring billing.

Cost shown as it lands. Every answer reports what it drew. Big jobs quote
before they run. A balance can never go below zero, so bill shock is
structurally impossible rather than promised.

Spending you can audit. The app already ships a Spending tab with a
thirty-day chart, totals by category, totals by conversation, and every
transaction individually inspectable. No subscription product has any reason to
build this screen.

### What we must not claim as unique

Two frontier AIs in one app is strong, but aggregators already offer multiple
models. All 11 official languages is strong for the interface, but the frontier
models already answer in isiZulu and isiXhosa, so this is fit rather than
exclusivity. Private chats matter, but temporary chat modes exist elsewhere.

Each of these is a reason to choose us. None of them is the one thing. Keep
them as proof, not as the headline.

## Q2. The audience and what they want most

> **South Africans who already use free AI, hit the wall often enough to be
> annoyed by it, and will not put a recurring charge on their card.**

The brand book names three groups. They are not equal, and treating them as
equal is how the copy ends up addressed to nobody.

**Primary. The free-tier wall.** They have used ChatGPT or Gemini or Claude on
a free plan. They know the product is good, because they have already had the
good answer. Then it stopped mid-task. The only route forward is a monthly
subscription, and they will not take one, whether that is lumpy income, a card
kept deliberately away from recurring charges, or one bad cancellation too
many. This is the volume, and crucially they need no education about what AI is
or why it is useful. That makes them the cheapest audience to convert.

**Secondary, and the sharpest wedge. The Claude gap.** They specifically want
Claude. It costs about R324 a month, in dollars, on a card that takes
international recurring billing, which many South Africans simply do not hold.
Small audience, very high intent, and the only real solution in the market is
ours. Use this group for launch messaging and paid targeting, not for the
homepage headline.

**Channel, not an audience. Partner reach.** Clicks Connect, Absa and Digital
Mobile put a bundle on a balance somebody already holds. This is distribution
strategy and belongs in partner-specific surfaces, not in the core positioning.

### What they want most

Not AI. They already have AI, for free, until it stops.

What they want is **to get past the wall without signing up for anything.**
Underneath that sits a want the category consistently ignores: control. Knowing
what they spent. No surprise debit order. No cancellation flow to fight
through. The freedom to spend nothing next month without having to do anything
to make that happen.

### What they are afraid of

Worth naming, because these are the objections the copy has to answer.

That prepaid means a stripped-down or second-rate AI. That R20 buys almost
nothing and they will be topping up constantly. That the cost will run away
from them. That their credit will vanish. That signing up will somehow become a
subscription anyway.

## Q3. The message that audience needs to hear

> **You already know it works. You hit the limit. Top up R20 and keep going,
> with no card, no subscription and nothing to cancel.**

The argument in the order they need it.

**Start where they already are.** They have hit the limit. Say that, plainly,
because recognition does more than persuasion. Never open by explaining what
AI is.

**Reframe the payment, do not apologise for it.** Top-up is how South Africa
buys airtime, electricity and data. It is the normal way to pay for things
here, not the budget option, and the copy should carry that confidence. We are
not the cheap alternative to a subscription. We are the version that does not
ask for a commitment.

**Then remove the fear, one line each.** Every answer shows what it cost. Your
balance can never go below zero. Nothing renews on its own. Big jobs quote
before they run.

**And answer the suspicion before it is voiced.** Full-strength Claude and
Gemini, chosen per question. Not a lite model. This one matters more than it
looks, because prepaid pricing invites exactly this doubt and the whole
proposition dies if the reader concludes they are buying a downgrade.

### The line that does the most work

> **Nothing renews, so there is nothing to cancel.**

For the person carrying one bad cancellation too many, this is the sentence
that lands. It is not a promise or a policy, it is a description of how the
product is built, which makes it unarguable.

### Message hierarchy

Four pillars. Every section, feature and CTA on the site sits under one of
them. If something fits none, it does not go on the site.

**Pillar 1. Nothing to cancel.**
The payment model. Prepaid in rand, no card, no debit order, nothing renews,
balance never below zero. Thirty-day validity stated plainly rather than in
fine print.

**Pillar 2. Nothing hidden.**
Money and data both. Cost shown as it lands, quotes before big jobs, spending
auditable day by day and conversation by conversation, receipts held on your
phone. Private chats kept nowhere and never used for training, enforced on the
server rather than promised in a policy.

**Pillar 3. Full strength, not a lite version.**
The product. This is where the feature mass goes, and its job is to destroy the
suspicion that prepaid means less. Two frontier AIs, web search, photos,
documents and decks, deep research, shopping in rand, lists, collections,
shared chats and games.

**Pillar 4. Built for here.**
The fit. All 11 official languages, an entry-level Android from 2017, rand
pricing, and distribution through brands people already pay.

### Headline candidates

Working options, all traceable to Q1. The brand book's own line is the
incumbent and it is good.

- Prepaid AI, priced per answer *(brand book tagline, current front-runner)*
- Frontier AI, bought like airtime
- Ask anything. See what it cost.
- The good AI, without the debit order.
- Top up. Ask. See what every answer cost.

### Voice rules that apply to all of it

South African English. Rand as R2 495,00. Answer first, then detail, never a
paragraph of throat-clearing before the substance. Money always plain: what it
costs, what it drew, what is left. No hype words. Claim only what the product
does today, in numbers where numbers exist.

## Q4. The offer

> **Start free on a verified number. No card. Then R20 when you want more, and
> nothing renews, so there is nothing to cancel.**

The offer is not a discount, and it should not be. It is the removal of every
risk at once, and each removal is structural rather than promised, which is
what makes it hard to say no to and impossible to disbelieve.

| Risk removed | How | Why it is credible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paying before you know | Free grant on a verified number | Already in the product |
| Needing a card | No card to start | Nothing to enter |
| Getting locked in | Nothing renews on its own | Not a policy, it is the architecture |
| Bill shock | Balance can never go below zero | Enforced server-side |
| A big job surprising you | Quotes before it runs | Already in the product |
| Your chats being used | Kept nowhere, never trained on, erased when ended | Enforced on the server |
| Fighting a cancellation | There is nothing to cancel | There is no subscription to end |

Seven objections, none answered with a promise. That is the whole strength of
it.

### The comparison to put beside it

A month of Claude Pro at about R324 costs roughly the same as **16 Starter
bundles**. The cheapest subscription alternative at about R149 is about **7
Starter bundles**. If you ask a handful of questions a week, you are currently
paying for a hundred you never asked.

Use this. It is the single most persuasive arithmetic available, and it is
checkable. Competitor pricing was confirmed 28 August 2026 and needs
re-checking before launch, because the entire argument rests on it.

### The bundles, with the real figures

From the app's own Top up screen, 09 September 2026.

| Bundle | Price | Credits | Answers, app estimate | Credits per rand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | R20,00 | 60 000 | about 468 | 3 000 |
| Regular | R50,00 | 165 000 | about 1 289 | 3 300 |
| Heavy | R120,00 | 440 000 | about 3 437 | 3 667 |

All three price at about 128 credits per answer, and the header balance agrees
with that, so the estimates are internally consistent. Bigger bundles buy more
credits per rand, which is a plain argument we can make without dressing it up.

### Two problems with the offer as it stands

**In-app top-up is not built.** The Top up screen carries its own notice:
buying credit needs App Store products and receipt checking, which are not
built yet. So the site cannot currently send anyone to buy a bundle. Until that
ships, the honest primary CTA is the free grant, and the bundle prices are
context for what happens next rather than a purchase.

**The answer estimate may not survive contact.** Starter advertises about 468
answers at 128 credits each. The one real answer in the app's Spending log cost
861 credits, which would make Starter about 70 answers. One data point, on what
looks like a long research answer, so it may well be an outlier. But a brand
built on showing every cost cannot put "about 468 answers" on a homepage
without knowing the real spread. Get the distribution before that number goes
anywhere near the site.

### The objection we have to answer, not dodge

Credit is valid 30 days and lapses. Someone will ask whether their money
disappears, and burying it would contradict the one thing we are selling.

Answer it plainly and early: credit is valid 30 days, the same as airtime, and
we would rather tell you here than in fine print. Whether 30 days is the right
number, or whether unused credit should roll over into the next top-up, is a
product decision worth taking seriously, because it is the only soft spot in an
otherwise airtight offer.

### Notes on the awkward ones

**Games are the surprise, and they need care.** Quiz Night, Twenty Questions,
Stop the Bus and The Court, with an AI referee keeping score, are genuinely
unusual and appear nowhere in the brand book. They are also the feature most
likely to make a serious buyer think this is a toy. Put them late, frame them
as proof that a R20 bundle buys a full product, and never let them near the
hero.

**Shared chats carry a money story, not just a social one.** Up to five people,
everybody reads every answer, and the cost is split between whoever pays. That
last part belongs to Pillar 1 as much as Pillar 3. The constraint is worth
stating too: a chat is shared from the moment it starts or never, which means a
private conversation can never be retroactively exposed.

**Lists and Collections are the anti-toy evidence.** The dinner list came back
with seventeen properly specified grocery items, quantities included. That is
the kind of concrete demonstration that does more than an adjective.

**The Spending tab is the single best proof we have** of the one thing from Q1,
and it is a screenshot rather than a claim. Whatever else the site does, it
should show that screen.

## Open decisions

Things the copy cannot be finished without. Each one changes what gets written.

**Is the free-tier audience the primary?** The recommendation above says yes,
with the Claude gap as the launch wedge. This is a business call, not a
copywriting one, and everything in Q3 changes if the answer is different.

**How big is the free grant?** The brand book confirms a grant exists but not
its size. The offer in Q4 is built on it, so the number is needed before the
CTA can be written.

**When does in-app top-up ship?** Until it does, the site can only offer the
free start. This determines the primary CTA.

**What does an answer really cost?** 128 credits by the app's estimate, 861 in
the one logged transaction. Need the distribution before any answer-count claim
goes on the site.

**Is 30-day lapse fixed?** The only soft spot in the offer. Worth asking
whether unused credit could roll into the next top-up.

**Six languages or eleven?** Still open from `ASSUMPTIONS.md`. The brand book
says both, in different sections. Pillar 4 currently claims 11.

**Does privacy deserve its own pillar?** It sits inside Pillar 2 as a
sub-proof. If research says trust is a bigger objection than cost clarity, it
should be promoted to a fifth pillar.

**Which decimal separator wins?** The brand book writes R2 495,00 and the app
writes R20.00. The site follows the brand book. One of the two has to move.
