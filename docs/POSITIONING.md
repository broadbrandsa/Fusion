# Positioning and Messaging

The messaging spine for Digital Fusion, and the frame every feature has to fit.
Nothing goes on the site that does not ladder back to the four answers below.

**How to use this.** The body reads as current truth. History lives in the
decisions table and the changelog at the bottom. When something changes, change
the body and add a line to the changelog, rather than annotating the body with
dates. Sources are the Brand Book v1.1 (01 September 2026), seven app
screenshots (09 September 2026) and team input (04 and 09 September 2026).

---

## The problem we solve

People use AI in bursts. A renovation, a business plan, a thesis, a month of job
applications. Heavy for a fortnight, then almost nothing for a month. Every
product on the market bills them flat. So they either pay through the quiet
stretches for questions they never ask, or they subscribe, cancel, resubscribe,
and fight the cancellation flow every time.

And they cannot tell how badly the shape fits, because a subscription hides what
any single answer costs. Under a monthly fee the question "what did that just
cost me?" has no answer. Without that answer, buying the right amount is not
possible. You can only guess, and the guess is always the subscription.

In South Africa two more things stack on top. The subscriptions are priced in
dollars, on cards that take international recurring billing, which many people
do not hold or deliberately keep away from recurring charges. And the free tiers
stop mid-task, so the wall arrives at the exact moment the work matters.

For schools and training providers the same problem takes a different shape.
One subscription shared by a whole class, so nobody works in their own account,
nobody can see who did what, and the seat count never matches the class.

In one sentence: **people are buying AI in a shape that does not match how they
use it, and they cannot see the mismatch because the cost is hidden from them.**

## Q1. The one thing we can do that no one else can

> **Digital Fusion is the only AI you can buy for the burst, because it is the
> only one that tells you what each answer cost.**

Two halves, and the second is what makes the first worth anything.

Prepaid on its own is a payment method. Anyone could sell a voucher. But a
voucher for AI you cannot price is a lottery ticket, because you have no idea
whether R20 buys one answer or four hundred. Showing the cost as each answer
lands is what turns prepaid from a gamble into a purchase. You watch what a
project actually drew, and next time you buy exactly that much. The shape
finally fits, because you can finally see the shape.

That is how the one thing solves the problem above. Not by being cheaper, and
not by being more honest as a virtue. By making the right amount knowable.

### Why nobody can follow

A subscription cannot show per-answer cost, because under a subscription there
is no per-answer cost. The entire proposition of a monthly fee is that the unit
price stays invisible: a heavy user is carried by a light one, and revealing the
figure breaks that arrangement in both directions. So the competition is not
choosing to hide it. They cannot show it and remain a subscription. The moat is
the business model, not the technology.

Pay-per-use does exist, for developers, in dollars, on a card, through an API.
Nobody has put it in a consumer app, in rand, bought like airtime, with the cost
sitting next to the answer. That combination is ours alone, and it is checkable
in the product today.

### What holds it up

Prepaid, in rand, no card. Bundles from R20, bought like airtime, with no
subscription and no monthly debit order. The alternatives sell dollar
subscriptions from about R149 to R324 a month.

Cost shown as it lands. Every answer reports what it drew. Big jobs quote
before they run. A balance can never go below zero, so bill shock is
structurally impossible rather than promised.

Spending you can audit. The app already ships a Spending tab with a thirty-day
chart, totals by category, totals by conversation, and every transaction
individually inspectable. No subscription product has any reason to build this
screen, and its existence is the clearest evidence that we are a different kind
of thing.

### What we must not claim as unique

Two frontier AIs in one app is strong, but aggregators already offer multiple
models. All 11 official languages is strong for the interface, but the frontier
models already answer in isiZulu and isiXhosa, so this is fit rather than
exclusivity. Private chats matter, but temporary chat modes exist elsewhere.

Each of these is a reason to choose us. None is the one thing. They are proof,
and they go under the pillars, never in the headline.

## Q2. Who we serve and what they want most

> **People whose AI use comes in bursts, and institutions that need to buy AI
> per person rather than per month.**

The audience is defined by usage pattern, not by payment blocker. That
distinction does real work. It keeps the brand off budget-option ground, which
the brand book forbids, because the argument stops being "you cannot afford a
subscription" and becomes "a subscription is the wrong shape for how you use
this." That lands the same on someone earning R8 000 a month and someone earning
R80 000. Both are paying for a hundred questions when they asked fifteen.

### Consumers, the primary audience

Three groups, all bursty, all badly served by a monthly fee.

**Project and burst users.** They need a lot of AI for a fortnight and then
almost none. They want to buy enough to finish the thing, not enter a
relationship with a vendor. This is the sharpest consumer framing we have and
it leads.

**The free-tier wall.** They have used a free plan, already had the good answer,
and hit the limit mid-task. The only route forward is a subscription they will
not take on: lumpy income, a card kept away from recurring charges, or one bad
cancellation too many. The biggest volume, and they need no education about
what AI is.

**The Claude gap.** They specifically want Claude. About R324 a month, in
dollars, on a card that takes international recurring billing, which many South
Africans do not hold. Small, very high intent, and we are the only real answer.
Best used for launch and paid targeting.

### Education and training, the second audience

Schools, colleges and training providers who want to teach people to use AI
properly. Today they buy one subscription and make a class share it, which
breaks in every direction: learners cannot do individual work, nobody can see
who did what, and the seat count never matches the class size.

Prepaid fixes the shape. Buy each learner their own account with R50 preloaded.
Every learner works in their own account, the spend is capped per person by
definition, and nothing renews when the course ends. No shared login, no
per-seat monthly commitment, no procurement conversation about recurring
international billing.

**Blocked on product, but not absent from the site.** The screenshots show a
single-user app. There is no sign of bulk provisioning, an organisation or admin
view, or per-learner reporting. Shared chats up to five people is adjacent but
not the same thing. So the site makes no education claim. What it carries
instead is a route: one short section or footer link, along the lines of
**"Buying for a school or a team? Talk to us."** That captures the demand
without describing a feature that does not exist, and it tells us how real the
demand is before anything gets built. Somebody has to answer those enquiries,
and where they land is an open item.

**Partner reach** through Clicks Connect, Absa and Digital Mobile is a channel
that serves all of these. It is not an audience and it belongs in partner
surfaces.

### What they want most

Consumers want enough AI to finish the job, and then silence. Paid for only when
used, nothing running in the background. Underneath that sits control: knowing
what they spent, no surprise debit order, and the freedom to spend nothing next
month without doing anything to make that happen.

Institutions want each learner in their own account, a cost they set per head,
and no commitment that outlives the course.

### What they are afraid of

The objections the copy has to answer before they are voiced. That prepaid
means a stripped-down or second-rate AI. That R20 buys almost nothing and they
will be topping up constantly. That the cost will run away from them. That
their credit will vanish. That signing up will somehow become a subscription
anyway.

## Q3. The message they need to hear

> **You use AI in bursts. Pay for it that way. Buy what the project needs, see
> what every answer cost, and pay nothing when you are not asking.**

### The argument, in the order they need it

**Start with recognition, not explanation.** They have a project, or they hit
the limit. Name the burst pattern plainly, because seeing your own habit
described does more than any persuasion. Never open by explaining what AI is.

**Reframe the payment with confidence.** Top-up is how South Africa buys airtime,
electricity and data. It is the normal way to pay for things here, not the
budget option. We are not the cheap alternative to a subscription. We are the
version that fits how you actually use it.

**Show them they can see the shape.** Every answer shows what it cost. This is
the line that connects the payment to the problem: you can buy the right amount
because you can see what the last amount did.

**Remove the fears, one line each.** Your balance can never go below zero.
Nothing renews on its own. Big jobs quote before they run.

**Kill the suspicion before it forms.** Full-strength Claude and Gemini, chosen
per question. Not a lite model. Prepaid pricing invites exactly this doubt and
the whole proposition dies if the reader concludes they are buying a downgrade,
so it goes early and it goes plainly.

### The line that does the most work

> **Nothing renews, so there is nothing to cancel.**

For the person carrying one bad cancellation too many, this is the sentence
that lands. It is not a promise or a policy. It describes how the product is
built, which makes it unarguable.

### Working lines, all traceable to the argument

- Buy the amount of AI your project needs.
- You use AI in bursts. Pay for it that way.
- You don't use AI every day. Why rent it by the month?
- Pay for the questions you actually ask.
- Heavy for a fortnight, quiet for a month. Pay like it.
- AI when you need it. Nothing when you don't.
- See what every answer cost. Then buy exactly that much.

For education, once the product supports it:

- Give every learner their own AI, not a shared login.
- R50 of AI per learner. Nothing renews when the course ends.

### Message hierarchy

Four pillars. Every section, feature and CTA on the site sits under one of
them. If something fits none, it does not go on the site.

**Pillar 1. Nothing to cancel.**
The payment model, and the shape argument under it. Flat billing does not fit
bursty use, so buy the amount your project needs and nothing more. Prepaid in
rand, no card, no debit order, nothing renews, balance never below zero.
Thirty-day validity stated plainly rather than in fine print.

**Pillar 2. Nothing hidden.**
Money and data both. Cost shown as it lands, quotes before big jobs, spending
auditable day by day and conversation by conversation, receipts held on your
phone. Private chats kept nowhere, never used for training, enforced on the
server rather than promised in a policy. This pillar is where Q1 lives.

**Pillar 3. Full strength, not a lite version.**
The product. This is where the feature mass goes, and its job is to destroy the
suspicion that prepaid means less. Two frontier AIs, web search, photos,
documents and decks, deep research, shopping in rand, lists, collections,
shared chats and games.

**Pillar 4. Built for here.**
The fit. All 11 official languages, an entry-level Android from 2017, rand
pricing, and distribution through brands people already pay.

### The headline

The brand book tagline carries the mechanism. The strongest line lands at the
end of the subhead, where the reader already knows what the product is and the
line answers the objection they were about to raise.

> # Prepaid AI, priced per answer
>
> Top up like airtime, ask in your own language, and see what every answer cost.
> No card, no subscription, nothing to cancel.

Rejected alternatives, and why. "Nothing to cancel" alone says nothing about
what the product is, so it cannot be the H1. "Ask anything. See what it cost."
drops the prepaid framing. "The good AI, without the debit order" leans on a
competitor to make sense, which is fine in a section and too combative in a
hero.

## Q4. The offer they cannot say no to

> **Start free on a verified number. No card. Then R20 when you want more, and
> nothing renews, so there is nothing to cancel.**

Not a discount, and it should not be. The offer removes every risk at once, and
every removal is structural rather than promised, which is what makes it hard
to refuse and impossible to disbelieve.

| Risk removed | How | Why it is credible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paying before you know | Free grant on a verified number | Already in the product |
| Needing a card | No card to start | Nothing to enter |
| Getting locked in | Nothing renews on its own | Not a policy, the architecture |
| Bill shock | Balance can never go below zero | Enforced server-side |
| A big job surprising you | Quotes before it runs | Already in the product |
| Your chats being used | Kept nowhere, never trained on, erased when ended | Enforced on the server |
| Fighting a cancellation | There is nothing to cancel | There is no subscription to end |

Seven objections, none answered with a promise.

### The comparison beside it

**No competitor is named on the site.** The arithmetic is kept, the brand is
dropped, and the reader is trusted to know which subscription they were looking
at.

> A typical AI subscription costs R149 to R324 a month, in dollars, on a card.
> That is 7 to 16 Starter bundles. If you ask a handful of questions a week, you
> are paying for a hundred you never asked.

This is the most persuasive arithmetic we have, and it stays checkable without
a name attached: the range is real, the date it was checked sits beside it, and
anyone can confirm it in two minutes. Naming would add bite, but Claude is also
a model we sell, and the awkwardness of attacking a partner's pricing on our own
homepage outweighs the gain.

Competitor pricing was confirmed 28 August 2026 and must be re-checked before
launch. The internal version of this document may name names freely. The site
never does.

### The bundles

From the app's own Top up screen, 09 September 2026.

| Bundle | Price | Credits | Answers, app estimate | Credits per rand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | R20,00 | 60 000 | about 468 | 3 000 |
| Regular | R50,00 | 165 000 | about 1 289 | 3 300 |
| Heavy | R120,00 | 440 000 | about 3 437 | 3 667 |

All three price at about 128 credits per answer, and the header balance agrees,
so the estimates are internally consistent. Bigger bundles buy more credits per
rand, which is a plain argument we can make without dressing it up.

**Do not publish the answer counts yet.** The one real transaction in the app's
spending log cost 861 credits, not 128, which would make Starter about 70
answers rather than 468. One data point on what looks like a long research
answer, so likely an outlier, but a brand built on showing every cost cannot put
a number on the homepage that might be out by six times. Get the distribution
first.

### The primary CTA

**The app store download, promising the free start rather than a purchase.**

In-app top-up is not built, by the app's own admission. A reader who tapped "buy
a bundle" and landed on a screen saying receipt checking is not finished would
have caught us doing the exact thing we claim not to do. So the sequence has to
be: download, verify your number, use the free grant. Nobody meets the top-up
screen until the grant runs out, and the CTA and the first-run experience agree
with each other.

Wording: **Get the app, start free.** Never "Get a bundle" until top-up ships.
Bundle prices stay on the page as context for what comes after the grant, not as
a checkout. When top-up ships, the CTA needs no rewrite, only a purchase path.

### The 30-day lapse

**State it plainly and early, in the main flow, in our own voice.**

Credit is valid 30 days and lapses. Everyone understands airtime, so the concept
needs no defending, and burying it in an FAQ would contradict the one thing we
are selling.

> Credit is valid 30 days, the same as airtime. We would rather tell you here
> than in fine print.

Saying it ourselves turns the one soft spot in the offer into another proof of
Pillar 2. Rollover stays worth considering as a product change, but the copy no
longer depends on it.

### The education variant

Same structure, different buyer. Held for sales conversations until engineering
confirms bulk provisioning and per-learner accounts exist. On the site, only the
talk-to-us route appears.

> **Buy each learner their own AI. R50 a head, preloaded, and nothing renews
> when the course ends.**

Every risk a training provider carries gets removed: no shared login, no
per-seat monthly commitment, no recurring international billing to get through
procurement, and a spend capped per learner by definition rather than by
policy. A course that ends in March does not quietly bill in April.

### Notes on the awkward ones

**The Spending tab is the single best proof we have** of Q1, and it is a
screenshot rather than a claim. Whatever else the site does, it shows that
screen. The moment an answer lands with its cost beside it would be stronger
still, and we do not have a capture of it yet.

**Games are the surprise and need care.** Quiz Night, Twenty Questions, Stop the
Bus and The Court, with an AI referee keeping score, are genuinely unusual and
appear nowhere in the brand book. They are also the feature most likely to make
a serious buyer think this is a toy. Late on the page, framed as proof that R20
buys a full product, never near the hero.

**Shared chats carry a money story, not only a social one.** Up to five people,
everybody reads every answer, cost split between whoever pays. That last part
is Pillar 1 as much as Pillar 3. The constraint is worth stating too: a chat is
shared from the moment it starts or never, so a private conversation can never
be retroactively exposed.

**Lists and Collections are the anti-toy evidence.** The dinner list came back
with seventeen properly specified grocery items, quantities included. Concrete
does more than adjectives.

**Education is a demand story without a product story yet.** Nothing about it
goes on the site until engineering confirms what exists, because the whole brand
rests on claiming only what the product does today.

## How to write for Digital Fusion

The rules that fall out of everything above. Every piece of copy gets checked
against these before it ships.

1. **Name the problem before the product.** Recognition beats persuasion. Open
   on the burst, the wall or the shared login, never on what AI is.

2. **Argue shape, never affordability.** A subscription is the wrong shape for
   how you use this. Prepaid is the first-class way to pay, never the budget
   option. If a line only works for someone who is short of money, rewrite it.

3. **Connect the cost to the choice.** Seeing what each answer cost is not a
   virtue, it is what lets you buy the right amount next time. Say so.

4. **Every feature proves a pillar.** Describe it by the job it does for the
   reader. If it proves nothing, it does not go on the site.

5. **Claim only what is live.** `live: false` in `src/content/site.ts` means it
   does not appear. No roadmap language, no "coming soon" dressed as a feature.

6. **Prefer structural to promised.** "A balance can never go below zero"
   describes how it is built. "We will never overcharge you" is a promise.
   Always the first kind.

7. **Money is plain and visible.** What it costs, what it drew, what is left.
   Never a price in fine print. Written as R2 495,00, set in mono with tabular
   numerals.

8. **Say the catch ourselves.** Thirty days, stated early. It is the only way to
   play a real limitation, and it turns a weakness into proof of Pillar 2.

9. **Full strength, said early.** Pre-empt the prepaid-means-lite suspicion
   before the reader forms it. Claude and Gemini by name, chosen per question.

10. **Answer first, then detail.** No throat-clearing paragraph before the
    substance. The first sentence of any section carries its point.

11. **No hype words, and no unverified numbers.** Numbers where numbers exist.
    Answer counts stay off the site until the real spread is known.

12. **South African English, written by a person.** Rand, not dollars. Airtime,
    not top-up credits. Short sentences and long ones. It should read like
    someone who knows the product explaining it across a table.

13. **Never name a competitor on the site.** Compare by range and shape: "a
    typical AI subscription costs R149 to R324 a month." The internal documents
    may name names. The public site does not, partly because one of them is
    also a model we sell.

14. **Games go late and never near the hero.**

## Open questions

What the copy cannot be finished without.

**Facts still needed**

- The size of the free grant. The hero promises a free start and cannot say what
  you get.
- The real Apple and Google Play URLs. Bundle id is `com.dsg.vasai` on both.
- A screenshot of an answer landing with its cost beside it, and one of a big
  job quoting before it runs. These are the two strongest proofs of Q1 and
  neither is captured yet.
- The real distribution of credits per answer. 128 by the app's estimate, 861
  in the one logged transaction.
- Whether the product can serve education at all: bulk provisioning, an
  organisation or admin view, per-learner spend reporting. A straight answer
  from engineering before any education surface is designed.
- When in-app top-up ships.
- Where institutional enquiries go. The talk-to-us route needs a destination,
  an address or a form, and a named person who answers.

**Decisions still open**

- Is there a volume or institutional price, or does a school buying 200 accounts
  pay the standard bundle price? Defensible either way, but it should be a
  decision.
- Should unused credit roll over? No longer blocks the copy, still worth
  considering as a product change.
- Six languages or eleven? The brand book says both. Pillar 4 claims 11.
- Does privacy deserve its own pillar? It sits inside Pillar 2. If trust turns
  out to be a bigger objection than cost clarity, promote it.
- Which decimal separator wins? Brand book R2 495,00, app R20.00. The site
  follows the book.
