# Positioning and Messaging

The high-level messaging spine for Digital Fusion, and the frame every feature
has to fit into. Nothing on the site should say anything that does not ladder
back to one of the four answers below.

**Status:** first pass, 09 September 2026. Recommendations, not sign-off.
Sources are the Brand Book v1.1 and seven app screenshots dated 09 September
2026. Open decisions are collected at the end, and the changelog is at the very
bottom. Update this document rather than starting a new one.

---

## Q1. The one thing we can do that no one else can

> **You buy frontier AI the way you buy airtime, and every answer tells you
> what it cost.**

Two halves, and neither works alone. Prepaid on its own is just a payment
method. Cost transparency on its own is a settings screen. Together they are a
different product category, and the reason no competitor can follow is
structural rather than technical.

A subscription business cannot show you what each answer cost. The entire
proposition of a monthly fee is that the unit cost stays invisible, because a
heavy user is subsidised by a light one and revealing the per-answer figure
breaks the arrangement in both directions. So the competition is not choosing
to hide it. They cannot show it and stay a subscription.

That is the moat. Not the technology, not the model access, not the price. The
business model.

### What holds this up

Prepaid, in rand, no card. A R20 bundle bought like airtime, with no
subscription and no monthly debit order. The alternatives sell dollar
subscriptions from about R149 to R324 a month, on cards that take
international recurring billing.

Cost shown as it lands. Every answer reports what it drew. Big jobs quote
before they run. A balance can never go below zero, so bill shock is
structurally impossible rather than promised.

Spending you can audit. The app already ships a Spending tab with a
thirty-day chart, totals by category, totals by conversation, and every
transaction individually inspectable. No subscription product has any reason to
build this screen.

### What we must not claim as unique

Two frontier AIs in one app is strong, but aggregators already offer multiple
models. All 11 official languages is strong for the interface, but the frontier
models already answer in isiZulu and isiXhosa, so this is fit rather than
exclusivity. Private chats matter, but temporary chat modes exist elsewhere.

Each of these is a reason to choose us. None of them is the one thing. Keep
them as proof, not as the headline.

## Q2. The audience and what they want most

> **People whose AI use comes in bursts, and institutions buying AI per person
> rather than per month.**

Decided 09 September 2026, refined the same day with team input. The audience is
defined by **usage pattern, not by payment blocker**, and that distinction does
real work. It keeps the brand out of budget-option territory, which the brand
book explicitly forbids, because the argument stops being "you cannot afford a
subscription" and becomes "a subscription is the wrong shape for how you
actually use this."

### The insight underneath all of it

**A subscription bills flat. Real AI use comes in bursts.**

Most people use AI heavily for a week, or for one project, and then barely touch
it for a month. A monthly fee charges the same either way, so you overpay
through every quiet stretch, or you subscribe and cancel and resubscribe and
fight the cancellation flow each time. That mismatch between flat billing and
lumpy use is the whole opportunity, and prepaid is the only shape that fits it.

Said plainly: **buy the amount of AI your project needs, and nothing more.**

That is a better argument than affordability ever was, because it applies to
someone earning R8 000 a month and someone earning R80 000 equally. Both are
paying for a hundred questions when they asked fifteen.

### Consumer, the primary audience

Three groups, all bursty, all badly served by a monthly fee.

**Project and burst users.** A renovation, a business plan, a thesis, a month of
job applications. They need a lot of AI for a fortnight and then almost none.
They want to buy enough to finish the thing, not to enter a relationship with a
vendor. This is the sharpest consumer framing we have.

**The free-tier wall.** They have used a free plan, already had the good answer,
and hit the limit mid-task. The only route forward is a subscription they will
not take on, whether that is lumpy income, a card kept away from recurring
charges, or one bad cancellation too many. Biggest volume, and they need no
education about what AI is.

**The Claude gap.** They specifically want Claude. About R324 a month, in
dollars, on a card that takes international recurring billing, which many South
Africans do not hold. Small, very high intent, and we are the only real answer.
Best used for launch and paid targeting.

### Education and training, the second audience

Added 09 September 2026 from team input, and it deserves its own line rather
than a footnote.

Schools, colleges and training providers who want to teach people to use AI
properly. Today they buy one monthly subscription and make a class share it,
which breaks in every direction: learners cannot do individual work, nobody can
see who did what, and the seat count never matches the class size.

Prepaid fixes the shape of it. Buy each learner their own account with R50 of
credit preloaded. Every learner works in their own account, the spend is capped
per person by definition, and nothing renews when the course ends. No shared
login, no per-seat monthly commitment, no procurement conversation about
recurring international billing.

**This needs product validation before it goes anywhere near the site.** The
screenshots show a single-user app. There is no sign of bulk provisioning,
an organisation or admin view, or per-learner reporting, and shared chats up to
five people is adjacent but not the same thing. The demand story is strong and
the product story is unproven, so treat this as a route to develop rather than a
claim to make. See the open decisions.

**Partner reach** through Clicks Connect, Absa and Digital Mobile is a channel
serving all of these, not another audience. It belongs in partner surfaces.

### What they want most

Consumers want AI on tap, paid for only when used, with nothing running in the
background. Enough to finish the job, and then silence. Underneath that sits
control: knowing what they spent, no surprise debit order, and the freedom to
spend nothing next month without doing anything to make that happen.

Institutions want each learner working in their own account, a cost they set
per head, and no commitment that outlives the course.

### What they are afraid of

The objections the copy has to answer. That prepaid means a stripped-down or
second-rate AI. That R20 buys almost nothing and they will be topping up
constantly. That the cost will run away from them. That their credit will
vanish. That signing up will somehow become a subscription anyway.

## Q3. The message that audience needs to hear

> **Your AI use comes in bursts. Your billing shouldn't. Buy what the project
> needs, see what every answer cost, and stop whenever you like.**

The argument in the order they need it.

**Start where they already are.** They have hit the limit. Say that, plainly,
because recognition does more than persuasion. Never open by explaining what
AI is.

**Reframe the payment, do not apologise for it.** Top-up is how South Africa
buys airtime, electricity and data. It is the normal way to pay for things
here, not the budget option, and the copy should carry that confidence. We are
not the cheap alternative to a subscription. We are the version that does not
ask for a commitment.

**Then remove the fear, one line each.** Every answer shows what it cost. Your
balance can never go below zero. Nothing renews on its own. Big jobs quote
before they run.

**And answer the suspicion before it is voiced.** Full-strength Claude and
Gemini, chosen per question. Not a lite model. This one matters more than it
looks, because prepaid pricing invites exactly this doubt and the whole
proposition dies if the reader concludes they are buying a downgrade.

### Lines that come straight from the burst angle

Written against the Q2 decision, so they argue shape and value rather than
affordability.

- Buy the amount of AI your project needs.
- You don't use AI every day. Why rent it by the month?
- Pay for the questions you actually ask.
- Heavy for a fortnight, quiet for a month. Pay like it.
- AI when you need it. Nothing when you don't.

For education and training, once the product supports it:

- Give every learner their own AI, not a shared login.
- R50 of AI per learner. Nothing renews when the course ends.

### The line that does the most work

> **Nothing renews, so there is nothing to cancel.**

For the person carrying one bad cancellation too many, this is the sentence
that lands. It is not a promise or a policy, it is a description of how the
product is built, which makes it unarguable.

### Message hierarchy

Four pillars. Every section, feature and CTA on the site sits under one of
them. If something fits none, it does not go on the site.

**Pillar 1. Nothing to cancel.**
The payment model, and the shape argument that sits under it. A subscription
bills flat while real use comes in bursts, so buy the amount your project needs
and nothing more. Prepaid in rand, no card, no debit order, nothing renews,
balance never below zero. Thirty-day validity stated plainly rather than in
fine print.

**Pillar 2. Nothing hidden.**
Money and data both. Cost shown as it lands, quotes before big jobs, spending
auditable day by day and conversation by conversation, receipts held on your
phone. Private chats kept nowhere and never used for training, enforced on the
server rather than promised in a policy.

**Pillar 3. Full strength, not a lite version.**
The product. This is where the feature mass goes, and its job is to destroy the
suspicion that prepaid means less. Two frontier AIs, web search, photos,
documents and decks, deep research, shopping in rand, lists, collections,
shared chats and games.

**Pillar 4. Built for here.**
The fit. All 11 official languages, an entry-level Android from 2017, rand
pricing, and distribution through brands people already pay.

### Headline, decided

Decided 09 September 2026: use both the brand book tagline and the strongest
line together, rather than choosing between them. The headline carries the
mechanism, the line underneath carries the feeling.

> # Prepaid AI, priced per answer
>
> Top up like airtime, ask in your own language, and see what every answer cost.
> No card, no subscription, nothing to cancel.

The headline is already approved brand language and gets both halves of Q1 into
five words. "Nothing to cancel" then lands at the end of the subhead, which is
where it does the most work, because by then the reader knows what the product
is and the line answers the objection they were about to raise.

Rejected, and why: "Nothing to cancel" alone is the strongest sentence we have
but says nothing about what the product is, so it cannot be the H1. "Ask
anything. See what it cost." drops the prepaid framing. "The good AI, without
the debit order" leans on a competitor comparison to make sense, which is fine
in a section and too combative in a hero.

### Voice rules that apply to all of it

South African English. Rand as R2 495,00. Answer first, then detail, never a
paragraph of throat-clearing before the substance. Money always plain: what it
costs, what it drew, what is left. No hype words. Claim only what the product
does today, in numbers where numbers exist.

## Q4. The offer

> **Start free on a verified number. No card. Then R20 when you want more, and
> nothing renews, so there is nothing to cancel.**

The offer is not a discount, and it should not be. It is the removal of every
risk at once, and each removal is structural rather than promised, which is
what makes it hard to say no to and impossible to disbelieve.

| Risk removed | How | Why it is credible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paying before you know | Free grant on a verified number | Already in the product |
| Needing a card | No card to start | Nothing to enter |
| Getting locked in | Nothing renews on its own | Not a policy, it is the architecture |
| Bill shock | Balance can never go below zero | Enforced server-side |
| A big job surprising you | Quotes before it runs | Already in the product |
| Your chats being used | Kept nowhere, never trained on, erased when ended | Enforced on the server |
| Fighting a cancellation | There is nothing to cancel | There is no subscription to end |

Seven objections, none answered with a promise. That is the whole strength of
it.

### The education variant of the offer

Once the product supports it. Same structure, different buyer.

> **Buy each learner their own AI. R50 a head, preloaded, and nothing renews
> when the course ends.**

Every risk a training provider actually carries gets removed: no shared login,
no per-seat monthly commitment, no recurring international billing to get
through procurement, and a spend that is capped per learner by definition rather
than by policy. A course that ends in March does not quietly bill in April.

Blocked on product. See the open decisions.

### The comparison to put beside it

A month of Claude Pro at about R324 costs roughly the same as **16 Starter
bundles**. The cheapest subscription alternative at about R149 is about **7
Starter bundles**. If you ask a handful of questions a week, you are currently
paying for a hundred you never asked.

Use this. It is the single most persuasive arithmetic available, and it is
checkable. Competitor pricing was confirmed 28 August 2026 and needs
re-checking before launch, because the entire argument rests on it.

### The bundles, with the real figures

From the app's own Top up screen, 09 September 2026.

| Bundle | Price | Credits | Answers, app estimate | Credits per rand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | R20,00 | 60 000 | about 468 | 3 000 |
| Regular | R50,00 | 165 000 | about 1 289 | 3 300 |
| Heavy | R120,00 | 440 000 | about 3 437 | 3 667 |

All three price at about 128 credits per answer, and the header balance agrees
with that, so the estimates are internally consistent. Bigger bundles buy more
credits per rand, which is a plain argument we can make without dressing it up.

### The CTA, decided

Decided 09 September 2026: **the primary CTA is the app store download.**

This works, but only if the promise attached to it is the free start rather than
a purchase. In-app top-up is not built, by the app's own admission, so a reader
who taps "buy a bundle" and lands on a screen saying receipt checking is not
finished would have caught us doing the exact thing we claim not to do.

The sequence that keeps it honest: download, verify your number, use the free
grant. Nobody needs the top-up screen until the grant runs out, so the CTA and
the first-run experience agree with each other. Wording along the lines of
**"Get the app, start free"** rather than "Get a bundle".

Bundle prices stay on the page as context for what happens after the grant.
They are not a checkout. Once top-up ships, the CTA needs no rewrite, only the
addition of a purchase path.

Store links are not in the repo yet. The Apple listing is Digital Fusion AI and
both stores use `com.dsg.vasai`, but the actual URLs need supplying before the
CTA can be wired.

**The answer estimate may not survive contact.** Starter advertises about 468
answers at 128 credits each. The one real answer in the app's Spending log cost
861 credits, which would make Starter about 70 answers. One data point, on what
looks like a long research answer, so it may well be an outlier. But a brand
built on showing every cost cannot put "about 468 answers" on a homepage
without knowing the real spread. Get the distribution before that number goes
anywhere near the site.

### The 30-day lapse, decided

Decided 09 September 2026: **state it plainly and early.**

Credit is valid 30 days and lapses. Everyone already understands airtime, so the
concept needs no defending, and burying it in an FAQ would contradict the one
thing we are selling. It goes near the bundles, in the main flow, in the
product's own voice.

> Credit is valid 30 days, the same as airtime. We would rather tell you here
> than in fine print.

Saying it ourselves turns the weakest point in the offer into another proof of
Pillar 2, which is the only way to play a genuine limitation. Rollover stays
worth considering as a product change, but the copy no longer depends on it.

### Notes on the awkward ones

**Games are the surprise, and they need care.** Quiz Night, Twenty Questions,
Stop the Bus and The Court, with an AI referee keeping score, are genuinely
unusual and appear nowhere in the brand book. They are also the feature most
likely to make a serious buyer think this is a toy. Put them late, frame them
as proof that a R20 bundle buys a full product, and never let them near the
hero.

**Shared chats carry a money story, not just a social one.** Up to five people,
everybody reads every answer, and the cost is split between whoever pays. That
last part belongs to Pillar 1 as much as Pillar 3. The constraint is worth
stating too: a chat is shared from the moment it starts or never, which means a
private conversation can never be retroactively exposed.

**Lists and Collections are the anti-toy evidence.** The dinner list came back
with seventeen properly specified grocery items, quantities included. That is
the kind of concrete demonstration that does more than an adjective.

**Education is a demand story without a product story yet.** Per-learner
accounts with preloaded credit is a strong route and the prepaid model fits it
almost too neatly. But the screenshots show a single-user app with no bulk
provisioning, no organisation view and no per-learner reporting. Nothing about
it goes on the site until that exists, because the whole brand rests on claiming
only what the product does today.

**The Spending tab is the single best proof we have** of the one thing from Q1,
and it is a screenshot rather than a claim. Whatever else the site does, it
should show that screen.

## Open decisions

Things the copy cannot be finished without. Each one changes what gets written.

**How big is the free grant?** The brand book confirms a grant exists but not
its size. The CTA now promises a free start, so this number is needed before the
hero can be finished. This is the most urgent open item.

**Can the product actually serve education?** Per-learner accounts with
preloaded credit needs bulk provisioning, an organisation or admin view, and
per-learner spend reporting. None of that is visible in the app today. This is
the biggest question on the list, because the demand story is the strongest
thing to come out of this session and the product story behind it is entirely
unverified. Worth a straight answer from engineering before any education
surface gets designed.

**Is there a volume or institutional price?** The education offer above assumes
R50 a head at the standard Regular price. If a school buying 200 accounts pays
the same as one person buying one bundle, that is a defensible position, but it
should be a decision rather than an oversight.

**What are the store URLs?** The CTA is a download, so it needs real Apple and
Google Play links. Bundle id is `com.dsg.vasai` on both.

**When does in-app top-up ship?** It does not change the CTA any more, but it
determines when a purchase path can be added and when bundle prices can become
a checkout rather than context.

**What does an answer really cost?** 128 credits by the app's estimate, 861 in
the one logged transaction. Need the distribution before any answer-count claim
goes on the site.

**Should unused credit roll over?** No longer blocks the copy, since we now
state the lapse plainly. Still worth considering as a product change, because it
would remove the objection rather than answer it.

**Six languages or eleven?** Still open from `ASSUMPTIONS.md`. The brand book
says both, in different sections. Pillar 4 currently claims 11.

**Does privacy deserve its own pillar?** It sits inside Pillar 2 as a
sub-proof. If research says trust is a bigger objection than cost clarity, it
should be promoted to a fifth pillar.

**Which decimal separator wins?** The brand book writes R2 495,00 and the app
writes R20.00. The site follows the brand book. One of the two has to move.
